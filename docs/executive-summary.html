Executive summary

Value Story

A prompt skill that turns the documents about an AI initiative into a narrative for leadership — where every number can be traced to a document somebody actually read, and the gaps are named instead of filled in.

The problem it addresses

Initiative write-ups drift toward confident numbers with nothing behind them. Not through dishonesty — through ordinary rounding-up under deadline. A rubric score becomes a result. An assumption becomes a saving. A figure appears in a headline and nobody can say where it came from.

The cost lands later: a partner asks where a number came from in a room where the answer matters, and there isn't one.

Anyone can produce a confident page. The hard part is a page that admits what it doesn't know.

How it works

  1. Read the documents, and list what you read

    If you didn't open it, you may not cite it.

  2. Write the claim table before any prose

    Every number exists as data — with a tier, a source and an owner — before it exists as a sentence.

  3. Write the four chapters around it

    What problem existed · what capability AI unlocked · what outcome changed · why it matters to the firm. Only the third carries figures.

  4. Answer leadership's five questions, including the ones you can't

    An unanswered question is reported, never quietly dropped.

Claims are typed by the evidence behind them

This is the mechanism. A claim declares how strong its evidence is, and each strength looks different on the page — so a reader who never learns the vocabulary still sees that the third card is a different kind of thing.

The five questions it forces you to face

  • 01How much more productivity did we get?
  • 02How much risk did we reduce?
  • 03What new capabilities do we now have?
  • 04How much cost did we avoid?
  • 05How many employees and functions were enabled?

Risk and reach are the two that get missed. Cost and time sit on the surface of a status report; control-exception counts and headcount reach do not. The skill hunts for those two specifically — and where the evidence is silent, it says so.

"No baseline exists, so win rate is not claimed" is a finding, not a failure.

What it is, and what it is not

It does

Impose a discipline

Tiered claims, traceable figures, a fixed narrative arc, and the five questions answered or explicitly declined.

It does

Make the evidence gap visible

Most initiative records have a confident outcome sentence and no baseline behind it. This makes that specific rather than vague.

It does not

Enforce anything

It is a prompt. A narrative that ignores the rules looks identical to one that follows them. The discipline is the reader's to keep.

It does not

Supply the evidence

If the before-and-after numbers were never recorded, it makes that plain. It cannot fill the gap — and that is usually the real finding.

Why a skill, and not an application

Because there is nothing here to compute. No database, no state, no users to administer — the work is judgement, and the output is one document somebody writes once. An application would be a login wrapped around a page of rules.

Weight

One file against a codebase

The application version of this existed: twenty-one modules, a build step, a browser dependency, 389 tests. It was removed. The prompt does the same job at a fraction of the weight.

Integration

It meets people where they are

It drops into the assistant already pointed at the repository. No export, no connector, no second source of truth.

Change

A rule change is a sentence

Adjusting how a claim is judged means editing a line and saving. In an application it means a release.

The trade

No floor under a bad day

An application could refuse to publish. A prompt can only advise. That is the whole of what is given up, and it is worth it until other people start authoring these unattended.

Using it

One file, about 120 lines. No install, no dependencies, no account.

Claude Code, one project   .claude/skills/value-story/SKILL.md
Claude Code, everywhere    ~/.claude/skills/value-story/SKILL.md
Copilot in VS Code         .github/copilot-instructions.md
Anything else              paste it into the conversation

Then, in plain language: build a value story for this initiative from the documents in ./sources.

Five sample initiative packets ship alongside it, in portfolio-record format — including one built entirely as a trap, where a rubric grade moved from 2 to 5 between assessments and a draft steering slide already presents it as a result. If the skill refuses that, it will hold on a real record.
